# Chapter 17 — Traditional Construction under Modern Codes (現行法規と構造性能 / *genkō hōki to kōzō seinō*)

## Scope

This chapter explains how Japanese law treats buildings made with traditional joinery, and what research has found about how such frames behave. It covers:
- the Building Standard Law and its Enforcement Order provisions for timber;
- exemptions for heritage buildings;
- the calculation routes open to traditional frames;
- the research programme of 2008–2013 and the design manuals that came out of it;
- representative data on *nuki* joints, earth walls, *sashigamoi* frames and *ishibadate* sliding;
- fire, durability and energy regulations as they affect traditional houses;
- CNC precutting versus hand-cut joinery.

It ends with rules for designers who want to build traditionally today.

> **Important — this is not legal advice.** Building law in Japan changes often: major amendments took effect in 2000, 2007, 2016, 2019 and April 2025, and notifications (告示 *kokuji*) are amended more often still. The article contents summarised here were checked as far as possible against published summaries at the time of writing (2026), but **the reader must check the current text of the law, the Enforcement Order and the relevant notifications, and must engage a licensed architect (建築士 *kenchikushi*) and, where required, a structural engineer.** Items that could not be verified are marked "(verify)". Figures are representative.

The structural principles referred to here are set out in → Ch. 10. The historical and technical sources are in → Ch. 01.

## 1. The legal framework and its history

### 1.1 Structure of Japanese building law

| Level | Japanese | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Act | 建築基準法 *Kenchiku Kijun Hō* (Building Standard Law, Act No. 201 of 1950) | Principles, procedures (building confirmation 建築確認), categories of buildings, fire zones |
| Cabinet Order | 建築基準法施行令 (Enforcement Order, Cabinet Order No. 338 of 1950) | Technical provisions, including Chapter 3 Section 3 "Wooden construction" (Art. 40–49) |
| Ministerial notifications | 告示 (e.g. 建設省告示 / 国土交通省告示) | Detailed specifications and calculation methods referred to by the Order |
| Local ordinances | 条例 | Local additions and relaxations permitted by the Act |
| Related laws | 建築士法, 住宅品確法 (Housing Quality Assurance Act, 1999), 長期優良住宅法 (2008), 建築物省エネ法 (Building Energy Efficiency Act, 2015), 文化財保護法 (Cultural Properties Protection Act, 1950) | Qualifications, performance labelling, long-life housing, energy, heritage |

### 1.2 Timeline relevant to traditional timber construction

| Year | Event | Significance for traditional frames |
|---|---|---|
| 1891 | Nōbi earthquake | Imperial Earthquake Investigation Committee (震災予防調査会, 1892) begins promoting braces and iron straps for timber buildings |
| 1919/1920 | 市街地建築物法 (Urban Building Law) | First national building regulation, for designated cities |
| 1924 | Revision after the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake | Seismic provisions; bracing requirements for timber buildings introduced in the regulations (details: verify) |
| 1950 | Building Standard Law | Required wall quantity (壁量 *kabe-ryō*) for timber houses; sill anchoring; traditional *ishibadate* frames fall outside the standard specification rules |
| 1959, 1981 | Revisions | Required wall quantities increased (1981: "new seismic standard" 新耐震) |
| 2000 | Major revision (performance-based) | **Limit strength calculation** (限界耐力計算) introduced; connection specifications (Notification 1460); balanced wall placement (Notification 1352); foundation specification (Notification 1347) |
| 2003 | Amendment to Notification 1100 | Earth walls graded by thickness (wall factor 0.5–1.5) and other traditional wall types recognised (verify details) |
| 2007 | Revision after the structural data falsification scandal | **Structural calculation conformity review** (構造計算適合性判定, "*tekihan*") required for limit strength calculations, including small houses. It became costly and slow to build *ishibadate* legally, and traditional new-build fell sharply. |
| 2008–2013 | MLIT-subsidised research programme on traditional construction | Full-scale shake-table tests; draft design methods including *ishibadate* (§5) |
| 2016 | Order Art. 42 amended; Notification No. 690 | Post-to-foundation dowel (*dabo*) connection as an alternative to sill or anchoring |
| 2019 | Fire-zone amendments | New options for timber in quasi-fire zones (verify details) |
| April 2025 | Major revision | "Special exemption for Category 4 buildings" narrowed (4号特例の縮小); wall quantity and post size rules revised to reflect heavier buildings; energy-code compliance mandatory for all new buildings |

## 2. Enforcement Order provisions for timber buildings (Art. 40–49)

The following summarises the Order's "wooden construction" section (第3章第3節 木造). The wording is paraphrased; consult the current text.

| Article | Subject | Summary (paraphrase) | Relevance to traditional frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | Scope | Applies to timber structural parts; small buildings (e.g. tea rooms, arbours, and outbuildings ≤ 10 m²) are excluded | Small tea houses and pavilions are partly outside the section (verify limits) |
| 41 | Timber quality | Structural timber free of harmful knots, rot, grain deviation | Compatible with traditional grading (→ Ch. 03) |
| 42 | Sills and foundations | Posts at the lowest storey must have a sill below, except where (1) the post is fixed to the foundation, (2) in a one-storey building with *ashigatame*, or (3) since 2016, the post is joined to the foundation by a *dabo* or other method specified by the Minister, and it is verified that no harmful tension occurs in the post. Sills must be fixed to the foundation (with limited exceptions) | **Key article for *ishibadate*.** Two-storey *ishibadate* without dowels cannot use exception (2) |
| 43 | Posts | Minimum post dimension as a ratio of the vertical distance between horizontal members (table); reinforcement if more than 1/3 of the required section is cut away; corner posts of buildings with two or more storeys must be *tōshibashira* unless the joint is reinforced equivalently; slenderness ratio ≤ 150 | Post sizing; *tōshibashira* at corners (→ R10-043) |
| 44 | Horizontal members | No harmful notches in the lower side near mid-span of beams, girders, etc. | → R10-064 |
| 45 | Braces | Minimum brace sizes (tension braces ≥ 1.5 × 9 cm or ≥ 9 mm steel bar; compression braces ≥ 3 × 9 cm); ends fixed; no notching except for crossing braces with reinforcement | Applies only where braces are used |
| 46 | Required wall quantity | Walls or braced frames in both directions in sufficient quantity (壁量), with wall factors (壁倍率) for each type; balanced placement; *hiuchi* (horizontal braces) at floor/roof corners, unless structural calculations are made | **Main obstacle** for *nuki*/earth-wall frames using the specification route |
| 47 | Joints | Splices and connections of structural members must be fixed by bolts, cramps, pins (込み栓) or other methods specified by the Minister (Notification 1460) | Traditional pinned joints recognised in some positions |
| 48 | School buildings | Special rules for timber school buildings | — |
| 49 | Decay and termites | Anti-decay measures for posts, braces and sills within 1 m of the ground and termite measures where needed; moisture protection behind finishes | → R10-038, R10-041 |

Related provisions outside Art. 40–49:
- **Art. 22** — raised floor height ≥ 45 cm and underfloor ventilation, unless the ground is sealed (→ Ch. 10 §8).
- **Art. 36 onward** — general structural provisions.
- **Art. 82 onward** — structural calculation methods, including limit strength calculation under Art. 82-5.

### 2.1 Art. 42 and *ishibadate*

The 2016 amendment added a third exception to the sill requirement.
- The post may be connected to the foundation with a dowel under **Notification No. 690 of 2016** (平成28年国土交通省告示第690号).
- According to published summaries, the dowel is fixed into the foundation and embedded **at least 90 mm** into a post of at least **105 mm** width (or an equivalent dowel connection).
- It must also be confirmed, by a method specified in the notification, that **no harmful tensile force** (uplift) occurs in the post that would unseat the dowel.

In effect this allows posts on stone or concrete pads with dowels, with a calculation check of uplift. It does not by itself make a free-sliding *ishibadate* a specification-route building. Free sliding still requires the calculation routes (§4).

**R17-001** — **Must.** For an *ishibadate* building, establish at the outset which exception of Art. 42 (or which calculation route) the design will rely on. The choice fixes the base detail (free, dowelled or anchored; → R10-020) and the whole design procedure.

### 2.2 Art. 43 post sizes

The Order sets a minimum ratio of post dimension to the vertical distance between horizontal members (土台, 足固め, 胴差, 梁, 桁…). The long-standing table, before the 2025 amendment, read approximately as follows (verify against the current text):

| Building type | Posts in buildings with column spacing ≥ 10 m, or in schools/assembly buildings: top storey or single storey | …other storeys | Other posts: top storey or single storey | …other storeys |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (1) Buildings with especially heavy walls (e.g. 土蔵造 *dozō-zukuri*, earth-walled storehouse construction) | 1/22 | 1/20 | 1/25 | 1/22 |
| (2) Buildings with light roofs (metal, slate, wood shingles etc.) | 1/30 | 1/25 | 1/33 | 1/30 |
| (3) Other buildings (e.g. tiled roofs) | 1/25 | 1/22 | 1/30 | 1/28 |

*Example:* a tiled-roof house with 2,730 mm between sill and girder at the ground floor of a two-storey building: 2,730/28 ≈ 98 mm, so a 105 mm post satisfied the old table.

**2025 change.** Because modern houses have become heavier (insulation, solar panels, heavier glazing), the April 2025 amendment changed the method. For ordinary buildings, the required post size is now determined from the building's actual loads, using a formula or tables published by MLIT, instead of the simple light/heavy-roof classification. The heavy-wall (*dozō*) row is understood to remain as a ratio (verify). A heavy earth-walled, tiled traditional house will typically need **larger posts** than the old table suggested. The traditional 4–5 sun (120–150 mm) posts generally meet this, but it must be checked.

### 2.3 Art. 46 required wall quantity and wall factors

The **wall-quantity method** (壁量計算 *kaberyō keisan*) compares:
- the **effective wall length** in each direction and storey — the length of each wall multiplied by its wall factor (壁倍率 *kabe-bairitsu*);
- the **required wall length** — floor area × a coefficient for earthquakes, and the exposed elevation area × a coefficient for wind.

A wall factor of **1.0** corresponds to an allowable short-term shear of **1.96 kN per metre** of wall (200 kgf/m).

**Wall factors in Order Art. 46 Table 1** (long-standing values; verify current table):

| Wall / frame | Factor |
|---|---|
| Earth-plastered wall (土塗壁) or wood lath on one side (木ずり片面) | 0.5 |
| Wood lath on both sides | 1.0 |
| Brace of ≥ 15 × 90 mm timber or ≥ 9 mm steel bar | 1.0 |
| Brace ≥ 30 × 90 mm | 1.5 |
| Brace ≥ 45 × 90 mm | 2.0 |
| Brace ≥ 90 × 90 mm | 3.0 |
| Crossed braces (たすき掛け) of the above | 2 × single (90 mm crossed: 5.0) |
| Combinations | Sum, up to an upper limit (long 5.0; reported as raised in 2025 for combinations — verify) |

**Notification 1100 of 1981** (昭和56年建設省告示第1100号) specifies further walls and their factors, including panel walls. After its **2003 amendment**, **earth walls on bamboo lath** meeting specified conditions receive factors graded by thickness:
- the thickest specified earth walls (commonly cited as about **70 mm** total thickness) receive **1.5**, comparable to a single 30 × 90 mm brace;
- intermediate thicknesses receive **1.0**;
- others keep **0.5**.

The conditions concern:
- the *nuki* (number, section, spacing);
- the bamboo lath (*mawatashi-dake* diameter, *komai* spacing);
- the earth coats (*arakabe* and *nakanuri*);
- the thickness.

The same period also saw the recognition of other traditional walls in the notification, with their own factors and conditions:
- lattice walls (面格子壁 *mengōshi-kabe*);
- dropped-in thick board walls (落とし込み板壁 *otoshikomi-itakabe*);
- earth-plastered hanging walls and waist walls (垂れ壁・腰壁) under specified conditions.

Exact specifications and values must be taken from the current notification.

**Required quantities before 2025** (representative; cm of wall per m² of floor area, earthquake):

| Building | Light roof | Heavy roof |
|---|---|---|
| One storey | 11 | 15 |
| Two storeys: ground floor | 29 | 33 |
| Two storeys: upper floor | 15 | 21 |

For wind, the exposed elevation area × 50 cm/m² (general), with higher values in designated windy areas.

**2025 change.** Required wall quantities for earthquakes are now calculated from the building's actual weight (roof, walls, insulation, solar panels etc.) by formula or MLIT tables, not by the light/heavy roof table. In addition, **quasi-bearing walls** (準耐力壁 *jun-tairyoku-heki*) and **hanging and waist walls** (垂れ壁・腰壁) meeting conditions may be counted in the existing wall quantity, as set out in the amended Notification 1100. There are also reduction factors for tall storeys (e.g. storey height > 3.2 m) (verify details).

**Balance and joints.**
- Notification 1352 of 2000 requires balanced placement, checked by the **quarter-division method** (四分割法 *yon-bunkatsu-hō*).
- Notification 1460 of 2000 specifies brace-end connections and the **post-top/post-base connections**, either from tables by position or by the **N-value calculation** (N値計算).
- The tables include traditional options such as the long tenon with draw-pin (長ほぞ差し込み栓打ち) for the lowest demand levels. Higher demands need steel hardware (plates, hold-downs).

**Why this route is difficult for traditional frames.**
- *Nuki* frames are not credited at all in the Order table and have only limited recognition otherwise.
- Earth walls reach at most 1.5.
- A traditional house with few walls on the street side and around the garden (open plans with *engawa* and *shōji*) rarely satisfies the required wall quantity and the quarter-division balance.
- The design philosophy of the wall-quantity method — stiffness and strength at small drift — conflicts with the deformation-capacity philosophy of the traditional frame (→ Ch. 10 §11).

**R17-002** — **Should.** If the specification (wall-quantity) route is used for a traditional-style house, use recognised traditional walls (thick earth walls, lattice walls, board walls) to meet the wall quantity. Avoid adding stiff braces or panels to a *nuki* frame merely to reach the numbers, unless the whole lateral system is redesigned (→ R10-067).

## 3. Exemptions and special regimes

### 3.1 Cultural properties — BSL Art. 3

Article 3(1) of the Building Standard Law excludes from the Act's application:
- (i) buildings designated as **National Treasures, Important Cultural Properties** and similar under the Cultural Properties Protection Act;
- (ii) buildings with certain earlier designations;
- **(iii) buildings whose preservation is regulated under a local ordinance (条例) for cultural or historic buildings and which the designated administrative agency (特定行政庁) designates with the consent of the building review council (建築審査会)**;
- (iv) reconstructions of such buildings, under conditions.

Repairs to designated buildings are governed by heritage law and practice instead (→ Ch. 16). In practice, structural safety measures are still sought, e.g. seismic assessment guidelines for cultural properties issued by the Agency for Cultural Affairs.

### 3.2 Municipal ordinances for historic buildings

Using Art. 3(1)(iii), several municipalities have ordinances that let historic buildings that are not national cultural properties be exempted from the Act, on condition of an agreed safety plan (fire, evacuation, structural reinforcement):
- **Kyoto City:** the **Kyoto City Ordinance on the Preservation and Utilisation of Historic Buildings** (京都市歴史的建築物の保存及び活用に関する条例, 2012) is used especially for *kyō-machiya*. Kyoto also has the **Kyoto City Ordinance on the Preservation and Succession of Kyō-machiya** (京都市京町家の保全及び継承に関する条例, 2017). The latter is a conservation ordinance (e.g. prior notification of demolition), not a building-code exemption. The city publishes guidance on what can be done with *machiya* under and outside the Act.
- Other cities have adopted similar Art. 3(1)(iii) ordinances. Check the current list with MLIT or the municipality.

### 3.3 Preservation districts — BSL Art. 85-3

In **Important Preservation Districts for Groups of Traditional Buildings** (重要伝統的建造物群保存地区, *jūdenken*), BSL Art. 85-3 allows the municipality, by ordinance and with the Minister's approval, to relax certain provisions of the Act. Typical examples are setbacks, height limits, and some fire provisions for roofs and external walls. This makes it possible to repair or rebuild traditional facades in the historic townscape.

### 3.4 Small buildings and the 2025 amendment

Until March 2025, small timber buildings (two storeys or fewer, ≤ 500 m², ≤ 13 m high and ≤ 9 m to the eaves: "Category 4 buildings", 4号建築物) designed by a licensed architect were exempt from **structural review** at building confirmation (the 4号特例). The designer remained responsible for compliance, but the documents were not checked.

From **April 2025**:
- The category was abolished and split.
  - **"New Category 2"** (新2号): two-storey timber buildings and single-storey buildings over 200 m². These now require submission and review of structural (and energy) documents at confirmation.
  - **"New Category 3"** (新3号): single-storey buildings ≤ 200 m². These keep a reduced review.
- The threshold at which timber buildings need structural calculation was lowered from 500 m² to **300 m²** of total floor area.
- The height limit for simpler routes was raised to **16 m**.

(Summarised from published explanations; verify details.)

For traditional houses this means:
- The specification-route checks (wall quantity, post sizes, joints) of a two-storey house are now **actually reviewed**.
- The practical scope for informally "traditional" two-storey houses without calculation has disappeared.

**R17-003** — **Must.** Treat every new two-storey traditional house (and every single-storey house over 200 m²) as requiring full structural documentation for building confirmation under the post-2025 rules, whichever route is chosen.

## 4. Design routes for traditional frames

| Route | Legal basis | Suits traditional frames? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Specification route** (仕様規定: wall quantity, balance, joints) | Order Art. 40–49 with notifications | Partly. Frames on a sill with thick earth walls, lattice walls and hardware or pinned joints | Cannot credit *nuki* deformation capacity; open plans fail; free *ishibadate* excluded (Art. 42) |
| **B. Allowable stress calculation** (許容応力度計算) | Order Art. 82 onwards | Poorly | Elastic, small-drift method; traditional joints are semi-rigid and non-linear |
| **C. Limit strength calculation** (限界耐力計算) | Order Art. 82-5 (introduced 2000) | **Yes** — the main legal route for *ishibadate* and *nuki* frames since 2000 | Capacity-spectrum-type method using the building's non-linear load–drift curve; checks at damage limit and safety limit; can include base sliding. Since 2007 requires the *tekihan* conformity review, even for small houses, which costs time and money |
| **D. Time-history response analysis + Ministerial approval** (時刻歴応答解析, 大臣認定) | BSL Art. 20 | Yes, for large or special buildings | Expensive; used for large temple reconstructions and research buildings |
| **E. Heritage exemption** | BSL Art. 3 (§3) | Only for designated buildings | Not a route for new buildings (except reconstructions under Art. 3(1)(iv)) |

### 4.1 Limit strength calculation in outline

1. Establish the **load–drift relationship** (復元力特性) of each storey by summing the contributions of each element at each drift angle:
   - *nuki* joints, *sashigamoi* joints, *ashigatame*;
   - earth walls, board walls, lattice walls;
   - column rocking and bending.

   Values come from tests or from recognised element data in the manuals (§5).
2. Convert to an equivalent single-degree-of-freedom system and derive the **capacity curve**.
3. Compare with the **demand spectrum**, reduced for damping, at two limit states:
   - **Damage limit** (損傷限界): for rare moderate earthquakes. A storey drift limit of the order of 1/120 rad is commonly used for timber under this route (verify).
   - **Safety limit** (安全限界): for very rare strong earthquakes. Traditional frames are typically designed to safety-limit drifts of the order of 1/30–1/15 rad; the manuals set the limits and the conditions for using them.
4. For *ishibadate*, the manuals give methods for checking whether the base slides and for estimating its effect.
5. Check members and joints at those drifts (post bending at *tarekabe*, joint pull-out, *nuki* splitting).

**R17-004** — **Should.** Where the limit strength route is used, commission the structural design early (before the *itazu* is fixed). Budget time for the conformity review (*tekihan*). Use element data from the recognised manuals, or from tests on the actual joint details, rather than from generic values.
*Why:* The performance of a *nuki* or *sashigamoi* joint depends on its species, section, fit and pins. Data transferred from a different detail may be unconservative.
