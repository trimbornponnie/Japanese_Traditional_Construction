# The Rulebook of Japanese Traditional Construction
### Joinery, woodworking techniques and assembly rules of Japanese timber architecture
#### 日本伝統木造建築 — 構法・継手仕口・規矩・組立ての規則集

---

## What this book is

This is a working reference that gathers the **construction rules** of Japanese traditional timber building in one place, from choosing the tree to raising the ridge and repairing the building a century later. It covers:

- the **historical carpentry manuals** (木割書 *kiwari-sho*, 規矩術 *kikujutsu* texts, secret scrolls) and modern standards the rules come from (→ Ch. 01);
- the **systems of measure and proportion** carpenters design with (→ Ch. 02, 05, 12);
- **material rules** for timber and tools (→ Ch. 03, 04);
- the **joinery**: general principles, a catalogue of splice joints (継手 *tsugite*) and connection joints (仕口 *shiguchi*), and the pins and wedges that lock them (→ Ch. 06–09);
- **structural assembly**: foundations, frame, roof, bracket sets and building types (→ Ch. 10–14);
- the **order of erection** on site (→ Ch. 15), **repair and dismantling** (→ Ch. 16), and how all of this fits **modern Japanese building law** (→ Ch. 17);
- a **glossary** of the Japanese terms (→ Ch. 18).

## How the rules are written

Every normative statement is a numbered rule:

> **R07-012** — *Must.* A koshikake-kama splice in a sill is placed so that the female (receiving) piece is the one that is anchored.
> *Why:* …

- **Rule ID** = `R` + chapter number + serial number. Rules can be cited across chapters (e.g. → R06-015).
- **Strength:** **Must** = structural necessity or universally accepted practice; **Should** = strong customary practice with known exceptions; **May / Variant** = regional, school or period practice.
- **Why:** each rule gives its reason wherever the reason is not self-evident. A rule you understand is a rule you can adapt correctly.

Terms appear on first use as **English gloss** (日本語, *rōmaji*). Dimensions are given in traditional units first, with metric equivalents: 1 *shaku* (尺) = 10 *sun* (寸) = 100 *bu* (分) ≈ 303 mm.

## Caveats

1. **Traditions vary.** Carpentry knowledge was passed down orally (口伝 *kuden*) within lineages, and proportions and customs differ between schools, regions (Kansai/Kantō and others) and periods. Where this book gives one reading, it is a representative one; the chapter notes where others exist.
2. **Figures are representative.** Proportions and sizes are rules of thumb for understanding and first sizing, not structural calculations. Real buildings need engineering verification.
3. **Not legal advice.** Chapter 17 summarises Japanese building law as understood at the time of writing. Laws change (for example the 2025 amendments), so check the current law and consult a licensed architect (建築士 *kenchikushi*) before building.
4. **Learn with a master.** No book replaces an apprenticeship. These rules are meant to support hands-on practice, not replace it.
