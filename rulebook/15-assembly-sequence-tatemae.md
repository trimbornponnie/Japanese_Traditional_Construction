# Chapter 15 — Assembly Sequence: From Site to Ridge (建方と上棟 / *tatekata to jōtō*)

## Scope

This chapter follows a traditional timber building in order, from the empty site to a roofed frame and the finishing work that follows. It covers planning and setting out, the ground-breaking rite, foundations, workshop cutting and trial assembly, frame raising (*tatemae* / *jōtō*), the ridge-raising ceremony, weather protection, walls and re-wedging, interior carpentry, and the checks and tolerances at each stage.

It is procedural. The *what* of each member is in → Ch. 10 (frame and foundations), → Ch. 11 (roofs) and → Ch. 12 (bracket sets). The *how* of each joint is in → Ch. 07–09. Tools and the workshop process are in → Ch. 04. Tolerances quoted here are **representative** workshop and site practice. They vary by firm, by building type, and by applicable standards, which are named where known. Safety regulations are referred to in general terms; the current Japanese occupational safety rules (労働安全衛生法 and its regulations) must be followed on any real site.

## 2. Pre-construction: drawings, timber, story poles

### 2.1 Plan board and section heights — *itazu* (板図) and *kanabakari* (矩計)

The traditional working drawing is the ***itazu***: a plan drawn at small scale (commonly 1/20 in one common reading; practice varies) in ink on a thin board, often of sugi or hinoki plywood today.
- It shows the grid with grid addresses (番付 *bantsuke*, → Ch. 02), all posts, and beam lines at each level.
- It shows the direction and type of splices, and the positions of *nuki* and *sashigamoi*.
- The *tōryō* uses it to plan cutting and raising order.

The ***kanabakari*** (sectional height drawing) and the ***shakuzue*** (尺杖, story pole) fix every height in the building (→ Ch. 02, Ch. 04):
- stone tops or footing top
- sill
- *ashigatame*
- floor
- *nuki*
- *kamoi* (lintel)
- *nageshi*
- *dōsashi* (floor-level girder)
- *keta* (plate)
- beams
- ridge

**R15-002** — **Must.** The *itazu*, *kanabakari* and *shakuzue* are completed and checked against each other before any timber is cut. From then on they are the controlling documents in the workshop and on site; other drawings are subordinate.
*Why:* Carpenters mark from the story pole, not from paper drawings. A mismatch between drawings and story pole is resolved in favour of the story pole unless it is corrected first.

**R15-003** — **Should.** The *itazu* shows the **erection order** and the insertion direction of every principal joint (§10). This can be done with arrows or numbers, or discussed and recorded by the *tōryō* before cutting.
*Why:* The choice of joint determines the erection order, and a joint that cannot be assembled in sequence is only discovered on raising day if it is not planned.

### 2.2 Timber takeoff — *kidori* / *kihiroi* (木拾い)

From the *itazu* the *tōryō* makes the timber list (木拾い *kihiroi*):
- each member with its species, section, length, grade (e.g. 無節 *mubushi* knot-free, 上小節 *jōkobushi*, 一等 *ittō*), and visible or hidden face;
- for logs, the top-end diameter (末口) and curvature needed.

→ Ch. 03.

### 2.3 Timber procurement and seasoning

→ Ch. 03 covers species, felling season, drying and grading. Relevant to scheduling:
- Air-dried timber for large posts and beams needs **months to years**. Order it early.
- Kiln-dried (KD) timber reduces post-raising shrinkage but can have internal checks.
- Log beams are selected individually in the yard by the *tōryō*, who reads their curvature and decides where each goes (適材適所 *tekizai tekisho*, "the right timber in the right place").

**R15-004** — **Should.** Select and assign principal members (*daikoku-bashira*, *ushibari*, log beams, *tokobashira*) before detailed marking. Mark (*bantsuke*) them in the yard.
*Why:* Their individual shapes determine the heights and lengths of the members they meet (→ Ch. 03, Ch. 04).

## 3. Site rites and setting out

### 3.1 Ground-breaking rite — *jichinsai* (地鎮祭)

A Shintō rite asking the tutelary deity of the land (土地の神 / 氏神) for permission to build and for safety during the work. Buddhist and other forms exist. It is not structurally required but is still held for most new houses in Japan.

- **Timing:** before any groundwork, on a day chosen from the traditional almanac. Days such as 大安 *taian* are preferred and 仏滅 *butsumetsu* avoided; many families consult the local shrine.
- **Setting:**
  - A sacred space is marked by four green bamboo poles (斎竹 *imidake*) at the corners, linked by a sacred rope (注連縄 *shimenawa*) hung with paper streamers (紙垂 *shide*).
  - Inside is an altar (祭壇 *saidan*) with offerings (神饌 *shinsen*: rice, sake, salt, water, fish, vegetables, fruit) and a sacred tree branch (榊 *sakaki*).
  - A heap of sand (盛砂 *morizuna*) represents the site.
- **Sequence (typical):**
  1. Purification (修祓 *shubatsu*).
  2. Summoning of the deity (降神 *kōshin*).
  3. Offerings (献饌 *kensen*).
  4. Prayer (祝詞 *norito*).
  5. **Ground-breaking acts** (地鎮の儀) on the sand heap. In a common form, the designer cuts the grass with a sickle (刈初 *karisome*), the owner breaks the sand with a hoe (穿初 *ugachizome*, also called 鍬入れ *kuwaire*), and the builder levels it with a spade (鋤入れ *sukiire*). The assignment of roles and the tool names vary between shrines and regions.
  6. Offering of *tamagushi* branches (玉串奉奠 *tamagushi hōten*).
  7. Removal of offerings (撤饌).
  8. Sending off the deity (昇神).
  9. A shared toast (直会 *naorai*).
- **Buried talisman:** a sealed talisman or small set of objects (鎮め物 *shizumemono*) is often given by the shrine to be buried under the centre of the building during foundation work.

**R15-005** — **Should.** Plan the *jichinsai* before setting out and groundwork. Record the position where the *shizumemono* is to be buried (usually near the centre of the plan) and bury it during foundation work, below the finished ground.

### 3.2 Ground outline — *jinawa* (地縄)

The approximate outline of the building is marked on the ground with rope or lime lines (地縄張り *jinawa-bari*), from the site plan and boundary survey. Owner, designer and *tōryō* check the position relative to boundaries, setbacks, trees and sun. Adjustments are easy at this point.

**R15-006** — **Should.** Confirm boundary points and setbacks with the owner and designer on the *jinawa* before erecting batter boards. Moving the building afterwards means redoing the *yarikata*.

### 3.3 Batter boards — *yarikata* (遣り方)

The *yarikata* is a frame of stakes and horizontal boards set around the building, a little outside the excavation, on which the grid and datum heights are marked.
- **Stakes (水杭 *mizugui*):** set firmly at intervals (typically ≤ 1 ken) and at the corners. Their tops are traditionally cut in a V-notch (いすか切り *isuka-giri*). If a stake is struck, the damaged notch shows it and the heights must be re-checked.
- **Level boards (水貫 *mizunuki*):** nailed to the stakes with their top edge exactly at a chosen datum height, e.g. GL + a round figure. This height is set by *mizumori* (§3.4).
- **Grid marks:** the positions of all main grid lines (通り芯 *tōrishin*) are transferred to the top edges of the *mizunuki*, marked with nails or saw-kerfs, and labelled with the *bantsuke*.
- **String lines (水糸 *mizuito*):** stretched between the marks to reproduce the grid at any time. A plumb bob (*sagefuri*) transfers the lines to the ground, stones or footings.
- **Bracing:** diagonal braces (筋かい貫) keep the *yarikata* stable.

**R15-007** — **Must.** All grid lines and datum heights are fixed on the *yarikata* and **not** on the excavation or formwork. The *yarikata* stays in place, protected, until the foundation (stones or sill) is complete and checked.
*Why:* The *yarikata* is the site's master reference. Once removed, the only reference is the foundation itself.

**R15-008** — **Must.** Check the rectangularity of the grid with diagonals (対角 *taikaku*) and with a large 3–4–5 set square (大矩 *ōgane* / *ōgane-zashi*) before marking. The diagonals of each rectangular portion must agree within the tolerance of §15.

### 3.4 Water levelling — *mizumori* (水盛り)

*Mizumori* ("water heaping") is levelling by water.
- **Traditionally:** a long trough (水盛り台 / 水樋 *mizudoi*) filled with water, or bamboo or wooden pipes with vertical sight tubes at both ends, established a level from which marks were transferred to all stakes.
- **Later:** transparent hose levels (水盛り管, still used).
- **Today:** automatic optical levels and laser levels.

The term *mizumori* is still used for levelling in general, and *mizu* ("water") appears in *mizugui*, *mizunuki* and *mizuito* for the same reason.

- **Datum (基準点 / BM, bench mark):** a fixed point outside the work area, e.g. a kerb, a stake in concrete or a road manhole, recorded in the drawings. The building's ground floor level is defined relative to it (設計GL).

**R15-009** — **Must.** Establish a permanent bench mark outside the work area before levelling. Re-check the *mizunuki* against it at the start of each foundation phase.

### 3.5 Profile boards for earthworks — *chōhari* (丁張り)

*Chōhari* are profile boards used for earthworks and slopes (法丁張 *nori-chōhari*), drains and external works, showing cut and fill levels. In building work the terms *yarikata* and *chōhari* overlap regionally. In Kantō usage *yarikata* is for the building and *chōhari* for civil and landscape profiles. This is a representative distinction and not universal.

## 4. Foundation sequence

### 4.1 *Ishibadate* (posts on stones)

→ Ch. 10 §2–5 for the principles.

1. **Excavate each stone pit** (壺掘り) at the grid intersections from the *yarikata*. For wall lines with many stones, excavate trenches.
2. **Ground work (*jigyō*):**
   - Rubble, gravel and earth layers, rammed (*takotsuki* traditionally, compactor today).
   - Or a small concrete pad under each stone in modern practice.
3. **Set bedding stones (根石)** and the foundation stone (*soseki* / *tsukaishi*).
   - Place the stone centre on the grid intersection (plumb bob from the string lines).
   - Set the highest bearing point to the design height from the *mizunuki* (±2–3 mm).
4. **Mark the stones** with centre lines (芯墨) and *bantsuke*.
5. **Check:**
   - All stones against the grid (plan).
   - Levels.
   - Diagonals of every rectangle.
   - Let the stones settle (or wait for mortar pads to cure) before scribing.
6. **Scribing (*hikaritsuke*)** of posts to stones (→ Ch. 10 §5) is done either now, with each post brought to site in advance, or at the start of raising.

**R15-010** — **Must.** Stones are set and checked before any post is scribed to them. A stone must not be moved after its post has been scribed. If it is moved, the post is re-scribed.

### 4.2 Concrete footing and sill (*nuno-kiso* / *beta-kiso* + *dodai*)

1. **Excavation (根切り *negiri*)** to the design depth.
2. **Base (地業):** crushed stone (砕石) rammed, then a thin blinding concrete layer (捨てコン *sutekon*), on which the grid lines are marked (墨出し *sumidashi*) from the *yarikata*.
3. **Reinforcement (配筋 *haikin*)** and inspection (配筋検査), which is required by some warranty schemes and by building control stages in certain cases; check the current rules.
4. **Formwork (型枠 *katawaku*)**, and **anchor bolts and hold-down anchors fixed in position** to templates according to the carpenter's plan (→ R10-034, R10-035).
5. **Concrete pour (打設 *dasetsu*)**, often in two stages: base slab, then upstands.
6. **Curing (養生 *yōjō*):** keep moist and protected. Minimum curing periods depend on temperature and cement type; in cold weather protect against freezing. Follow the applicable specification (e.g. JASS 5 and housing specifications). Do not load early.
7. **Top levelling (天端均し *tenba-narashi*):** self-levelling mortar (レベラー) or trowelled mortar to bring the top of the footing within tolerance (§15).
8. **Stripping formwork, backfill.**
9. **Sill laying (土台敷き *dodai-shiki*),** usually the day before raising or in the morning of the raising day, by the carpenters:
   1. Mark sill lines on the footing top from the grid (芯墨 and 逃げ墨 offset lines).
   2. Lay the pre-cut sills beside their positions in order.
   3. Mark and drill anchor-bolt holes by transferring bolt positions from the footing to each sill.
   4. Place ventilating spacers (基礎パッキン) if used, at the positions required (under posts, at anchors, at joints).
   5. Lay the sills **in order of their splices**: the **lower piece (下木 *shitaki*, the female piece with the socket)** is laid first, then the **upper piece (上木 *uwaki*, male with the tongue)** is dropped in from above.
   6. Start from a reference corner. Lay the perimeter first, then internal lines.
   7. Fit corner and T-junctions (→ Ch. 08).
   8. Lay the horizontal angle braces (火打土台 *hiuchi-dodai*) if used.
   9. Tighten the anchor nuts with washers, not overtightened, so as not to crush the sill.
   10. Check level (±), line and diagonals.
   11. Lay the *ōbiki* and set *tsuka* if the ground floor framing is done before raising. This is common in modern practice, because it gives a working platform (床先行 *yuka senkō*).

**R15-011** — **Must.** Anchor bolts must be cast in, never pushed into fresh concrete after pouring. They must be positioned according to the carpenter's sill layout (clear of mortises and splice tongues, near the upper piece of each splice).
*Why:* → R10-035.

**R15-012** — **Should.** Lay sills in splice order: lower (female) piece first, upper (male) piece dropped onto it. At the reference corner, lay the piece that receives the others first.
*Why:* *Koshikake* splices are drop-in joints (§10). The laying order is fixed by the geometry of the joints.

**R15-013** — **Should.** Do not tighten anchor nuts so hard that the washer bites into the sill. Re-tighten once after the frame has been loaded and after the first drying period.
*Why:* Timber shrinks, and a loose sill rattles on its bolts. A crushed washer seat weakens the sill in shear.

## 5. Workshop cutting (*kizami*) and trial assembly (*karigumi*)

### 5.1 Cutting sequence

→ Ch. 04 for the full process. In outline:
1. Sorting and planing of timbers.
2. Marking (墨付け *sumitsuke*) from the story pole and *itazu*.
3. Cutting (刻み *kizami*), usually by member family: sills, posts, girders, beams, roof members.
4. Finishing (仕上げ, planing of show faces) is often done close to raising, to limit handling marks and discolouration.

Principal members are marked by the *tōryō* or senior carpenter; others by the crew under checking.

### 5.2 Checks in the workshop

**R15-014** — **Must.** Before a member leaves the workshop, check against the *itazu* and story pole:
- its *bantsuke* (address) on at least two faces;
- its length between shoulders (not overall);
- the heights of all mortises;
- the direction of each joint (which side is male/female, which end is *moto*);
- that no joint conflicts with another in the same post.

A member with a wrong joint is cheap to fix in the workshop and costly on the frame.

### 5.3 Trial assembly — *karigumi* (仮組)

*Karigumi* is the temporary assembly in the workshop (or yard) of parts of the frame whose fit cannot be verified from marking alone:
- complex hip and valley corners (→ Ch. 05, Ch. 11);
- bracket sets (*kumimono*, → Ch. 12);
- curved roof edges (*sori*);
- *sasu* trusses and log roof frames;
- joints of unusual members;
- repair splices (→ Ch. 16).

For standard house frames, *karigumi* is usually limited to difficult corners. In temple work a whole bay or the entire roof base may be trial-assembled.

**R15-015** — **Should.** Trial-assemble any part where three or more members meet in non-orthogonal geometry, all bracket sets, and any log-beam intersection. Mark the fit (合印 *aijirushi*) on the parts before dismantling.
*Why:* On site, adjustments to a complex joint happen on scaffolding, under time pressure, with a crane waiting.

**R15-016** — **Should.** Dismantle *karigumi* in reverse order and bundle members in the order in which they will be raised.

## 6. Preparing for raising

### 6.1 Scaffolding (足場 *ashiba*)
- **Traditional:** log scaffolding (丸太足場 *maruta ashiba*) of sugi poles lashed with straw rope, later with annealed wire (番線 *bansen*). It was erected by the *tobi* (鳶, scaffolders and riggers).
- **Modern:** tube-and-clamp (単管足場) or system scaffolding (くさび緊結式足場, "big-frame" 次世代足場).
- **Placement:** the exterior scaffold is normally erected around the foundation **before** raising, leaving clear the crane's lifting zone and the space needed to swing members in. In temple work a full scaffold with a temporary roof over the whole building (素屋根 *suyane*) may be built first (→ Ch. 16).

**R15-017** — **Must.** The exterior scaffold must stand clear of the frame line by enough to let posts be stood, spread and plumbed (typically ≥ 300 mm from the wall face; representative). It must be independent of the frame. Do not tie the scaffold to the frame until the frame is plumbed and braced.
*Why:* A scaffold tied to a frame that is still being plumbed resists the adjustments, or pulls the frame out of plumb.

### 6.2 Material delivery and staging

**R15-018** — **Must.** Deliver and lay out members the day before raising (or early the same day) in **order of erection**, on bearers off the ground, bantsuke visible, within reach of the crane or the raising crew. Cover them against rain and sun.
*Why:* Searching for a member during raising wastes crane time. Rain on unfinished shiraki leaves stains (§16).

### 6.3 Pre-raising meeting

The *tōryō* briefs the crew, the *tobi* and the crane operator on:
- the order;
- the signals;
- the load limits;
- the position of each team;
- and the day's goal (usually: ridge by afternoon, roof boarding by evening, or next day).

**R15-019** — **Must.** Every person on site knows the erection order and their role, and the hand signals for the crane, before the first lift. One person gives crane signals (合図者 *aizu-sha*).
